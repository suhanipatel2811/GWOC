Gratitude Journaling

Three simple prompts to shift your perspective today. Regular gratitude practice has been scientifically proven to improve mood, strengthen relationships, and build resilience.

schedule 3 min exercise science Evidence-Based

Why Gratitude Matters

Gratitude is more than just saying "thank you." It's a practice that rewires your brain to notice the positive aspects of life, even during difficult times. Research shows that people who regularly practice gratitude experience:

mood

Improved mood and reduced symptoms of depression

bedtime

Better sleep quality and faster sleep onset

volunteer_activism

Stronger relationships and social connections

shield

Greater emotional resilience during stress

Today's Gratitude Prompts

1

What made you smile today?

It could be something small—a warm cup of coffee, a text from a friend, sunlight through your window, or a moment of laughter. Let yourself remember the feeling.

Your Response:

2

Who are you grateful for and why?

Think of someone who's made a difference in your life—recently or in the past. What specific quality or action are you thankful for? Consider telling them.

Your Response:

3

What strength did you use today?

Maybe you were patient, creative, brave, or kind. Acknowledge a personal quality you brought to a situation, even if the outcome wasn't perfect.

Your Response:

Making it a Habit

event_repeat

Same Time, Every Day

Choose a consistent time—morning with coffee, or evening before bed. Consistency builds the habit.

check_circle

Start Small

Even one sentence counts. The goal is reflection, not perfection. Some days, bullet points are enough.

auto_stories

Review & Reflect

Look back at past entries during tough days. It reminds you that good moments exist, even when they're hard to see.
